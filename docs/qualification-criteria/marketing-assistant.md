# Qualification criteria: marketing assistant

Draft 1. To be reviewed and updated by the Innovista team.

## Summary

We are looking for small and mid-sized companies with an active marketing function that is thinly
staffed, where marketing support (a marketing assistant, or services that do a marketing assistant's
work) would be valued. The right lead markets itself visibly online, has a small marketing team or
none, and shows signs of growth that increase the marketing workload.

## Who to contact

In this order, when found on the company's own website:

1. Marketing manager or head of marketing.
2. Marketing assistant or marketing coordinator.
3. Founder, owner or managing director, for companies under 50 people.
4. Head of sales or business development, where no marketing role is listed.

## Criteria

Each criterion is judged Meets, Does not meet or Unable to verify, with the evidence quoted.

1. **Markets itself actively.** Evidence: a blog or news section updated within the last 12 months,
   newsletters, downloadable brochures, event announcements or links to active social accounts.
2. **Small marketing team.** Evidence: a team page with at most three marketing people, a single
   marketing contact, or marketing handled by the founder or sales team. Unable to verify when the
   site lists no team.
3. **Growth signals.** Evidence: new products or locations announced, open roles in marketing or
   sales, or recent expansion news on the company's own site.
4. **Sells to customers who can be reached through marketing.** Evidence: clear products or services
   with a public offer, not a purely internal or government-only business.
5. **Company size between 5 and 250 employees.** Evidence: the about page, the careers page or the size
   in the lead record.
6. **Marketing contact reachable.** A named person in one of the roles above, or a marketing or press
   contact address, is listed on the company's own site.

## Exclusions

A lead matching any of these is Not a target, whatever else it scores.

1. **Marketing, advertising and PR agencies.** They are competitors of a marketing support offer.
2. **Companies above 1,000 employees.** They have large in-house marketing departments.
3. **Businesses with no public offer.** Holding companies, internal service entities.
4. **Sectors the client does not serve.** To be listed by the client (see open questions).

## Evidence the system can check

All criteria can be verified from the company's own website except size, which often needs the
employee count in the imported lead data.

## What the fit score means here

- 80 to 100: markets actively, small marketing team, growing, and a reachable marketing contact.
  Call.
- 70 to 79: markets actively and is the right size, but team size or growth could not be verified.
  Email.
- 50 to 69: some marketing activity, unclear need. Review with the client.
- Below 50: no marketing activity or an agency. Archive.

## Open questions for the client

1. Is the offer a service (an outsourced or virtual marketing assistant), a product, or recruiting
   marketing assistants for these companies?
2. Which sectors and regions are in scope, and which are excluded?
3. Should companies with no marketing activity at all count as a need (they have no one doing it) or
   as a poor fit (they do not value it)? The draft treats them as a poor fit.
