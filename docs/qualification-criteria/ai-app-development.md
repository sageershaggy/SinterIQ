# Qualification criteria: AI engineers for app development

Draft 1. To be reviewed and updated by the Innovista team.

## Summary

We are looking for companies that build software applications with AI in them, or are about to, and
therefore employ or need AI engineers. The right lead is a product company or software studio with
its own engineering team, public signs of AI work (AI features in its products, AI or machine
learning job openings, case studies), and the authority to decide how that work is staffed or built.

## Who to contact

In this order, when found on the company's own website:

1. Chief technology officer or head of engineering.
2. Head of AI, head of data or machine learning lead.
3. VP or head of product.
4. Founder or managing director, for companies under 50 people.
5. Engineering manager or tech lead for the app teams.

## Criteria

Each criterion is judged Meets, Does not meet or Unable to verify, with the evidence quoted.

1. **Builds software applications.** The company sells or operates its own web or mobile apps, or
   builds them for clients. Evidence: product pages, app store links, a portfolio of delivered apps.
2. **Uses or plans to use AI in its apps.** Evidence: product pages naming AI features (assistants,
   recommendations, image or speech recognition, language models), AI case studies, or an AI section
   on the site. A generic "AI-powered" slogan with nothing behind it does not meet this.
3. **Has an engineering team.** Evidence: a careers page with engineering roles, a team page with
   engineers, or an engineering blog. Fully outsourced development does not meet this unless the
   company is itself the studio doing the building.
4. **Is hiring or growing in AI or software engineering.** Evidence: open roles for AI, machine
   learning, data or software engineers, posted within the last 12 months where a date is shown.
5. **Company size between 10 and 1,000 employees.** Evidence: the about page, the careers page, or
   the size in the lead record. Below 10 is usually too small to buy; above 1,000 usually has
   centralised procurement.
6. **Decision-maker reachable.** A named person in one of the roles above is listed on the company's
   own site, or a role-based contact address exists for engineering or partnerships.

## Exclusions

A lead matching any of these is Not a target, whatever else it scores.

1. **Staffing agencies, recruiters and freelancer marketplaces.** They place engineers rather than
   employ them for their own products.
2. **Large technology companies above 5,000 employees.** Hiring and vendor decisions are centralised
   and out of reach.
3. **Companies with no software product and no software services.** For example a retailer that only
   uses off-the-shelf tools.
4. **Universities, public research institutes and student projects.** Different buying process.
5. **Direct competitors of the client.** AI development agencies offering the same service, where the
   client sells AI engineering services.

## Evidence the system can check

Criteria 1 to 4 and 6 can be verified from the company's own website. Criterion 5 often needs the
employee count in the imported lead data; without it the lead is Unable to verify on size.

## What the fit score means here

- 80 to 100: builds apps, visible AI work, an engineering team and a reachable technical decision
  maker. Call.
- 70 to 79: builds apps with AI but size or hiring could not be verified. Email.
- 50 to 69: builds software but AI use is unclear. Review with the client.
- Below 50: no software product or no AI signal. Archive.

## Open questions for the client

1. Is the goal to sell AI engineering services to these companies, to recruit their AI engineers, or
   to partner with them? The criteria and the contact roles change with the answer.
2. Which regions and languages should be covered?
3. Are there preferred technologies (for example specific language model providers, mobile
   frameworks) that make a lead a stronger fit?
4. What is the smallest company worth contacting?
